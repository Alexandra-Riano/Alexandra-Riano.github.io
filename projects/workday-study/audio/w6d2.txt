Week 6, day 2. Notifications. [[slnc 1200]] Today's objective is Business Process Notifications, in the Business Processes domain. [[slnc 600]] In one line: Communicate progress without granting access or replacing required steps. [[slnc 1200]] Let's start with the mental model. [[slnc 500]] Notifications can trigger on process status or step entry/exit. Configure effective date, recipients, trigger, conditions, delivery, and content. Dynamic fields respect security; recipients do not gain access merely by receiving a message. [[slnc 1200]] Now, the distinctions you need to know. [[slnc 500]] Notification: Inform someone. [[slnc 700]] business process step: Require or automate work. [[slnc 700]]  [[slnc 600]] Here's how it shows up at work. [[slnc 500]] Email a hiring manager when Hire completes, using only fields that recipient is authorized to view. [[slnc 1200]] Task and report names to recognize: Add Notification, Maintain Email Templates, Mass Update Email Address Option, and Preview Notification. [[slnc 1200]] And the exam trap to watch for. [[slnc 500]] A notification is not an Approval or Action and does not confer access. [[slnc 1500]] Quick check. I'll read each question and the choices, then pause so you can answer out loud. [[slnc 1000]] Question one. [[slnc 400]] What can trigger a business process notification? [[slnc 700]] A: Only the completion of the whole process. [[slnc 450]] Take a moment. [[slnc 5000]] The answer is D: A process status change or a step's entry or exit. [[slnc 500]] Notifications fire on status or step events. [[slnc 1400]] Question two. [[slnc 400]] Does receiving a notification grant access to the event? [[slnc 700]] A: Only for managers. [[slnc 450]] B: No. [[slnc 450]] C: Yes, approve access. [[slnc 450]] D: Yes, view access. [[slnc 450]] Take a moment. [[slnc 5000]] The answer is B: No. [[slnc 500]] A notification informs; it doesn't grant access. [[slnc 1400]] Question three.
