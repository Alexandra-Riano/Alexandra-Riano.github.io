Today's objective is Termination, in the Staffing Setup domain. [[slnc 600]] In one line: End employment, clean up the worker's tasks and roles, and secure the template. [[slnc 1200]] Let's start with the mental model. [[slnc 500]] Termination ends the working relationship. Start it with Terminate Employee (worker Related Actions, then Job Change), through Submit Resignation (the Termination process must include the Review Worker Initiated Termination step), or through Severance. The termination date is the last day of employment; the worker shows as terminated just after midnight in their own time zone. Last Day of Work and Pay Through Date default to the termination date. You can record rehire eligibility, regrettable or not, a questionnaire such as an exit interview, notice periods, and attachments. Maintain Termination Categories holds customer-defined reasons with Involuntary, Retirement, Inactive, and Manager Reason flags. Typical steps: Manage Business Processes for Worker (reassign or cancel the worker's tasks), Assign Roles, and a Terminate User Account service step. Job overlap lets a replacement share the position for a time. No Show replaces termination for a hire who never starts. [[slnc 1200]] Now, the distinctions you need to know. [[slnc 500]] Terminate Employee: Initiated by a manager or HR. [[slnc 700]] Submit Resignation: Worker-initiated, needs the Review Worker Initiated Termination step. [[slnc 700]] Manage Business Processes for Worker: Reassign or cancel the leaver’s tasks. [[slnc 700]] No Show: Hire never started, not counted in headcount.
