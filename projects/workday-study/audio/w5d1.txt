Today's objective is Define  and  Describe Organizations, in the Organizations domain. [[slnc 600]] In one line: Use organization types as distinct lenses for management, legal, costing, pay, and reporting needs. [[slnc 1200]] Let's start with the mental model. [[slnc 500]] The supervisory organization groups workers into the management hierarchy and holds positions and job restrictions; every worker belongs to exactly one. Workers usually also belong to a company (legal entity, tax ID), cost center (revenue and expense), region (area of responsibility), pay group (payroll), and location, and can be in several matrix organizations (dotted-line manager). Create custom organization types only when delivered types do not fit. Fields when creating an organization: Availability Date (when it appears in prompts), Name, Code, Subtype (tenanted; can enforce a subtype hierarchy), Visibility, and roles; supervisory organizations add External URL, Primary Location (required), Staffing Model, and Identifiers. Subordinates inherit Visibility, Primary Location, Staffing Model, and Roles. Maintain Organization Types groups types into Costing, Hierarchy, HR, Pay, and Custom. [[slnc 1200]] Now, the distinctions you need to know. [[slnc 500]] Supervisory org colon Who manages whom, and where staffing happens. [[slnc 700]] Other organization types: Legal, costing, pay, location, or customer-defined views. [[slnc 700]] Inherited by subordinates: Visibility, Primary Location, Staffing Model, Roles. [[slnc 700]] Visibility options: Everyone, Role Assignees, Role Assignees and Members, Role Assignees of Current and Superiors. [[slnc 700]]  [[slnc 600]] Here's how it shows up at work.
