[[slnc 1000]] This week's goal: Connect organization preparation to Hire, Change Job, and Termination. [[slnc 1200]] Recap: Staffing Business Processes. [[slnc 500]] Prepare first. A Position Management organization needs an open position (Create Position business process); a Job Management organization needs hiring restrictions (Hiring Restrictions business process). Until then, Hire does not appear in the organization's Related Actions. Hire works the same under both models. It records hire date, position, employee type, job profile, time type, and location. Other values default: management level, job classification, and work shift from the job profile, and weekly hours from the location. When a position and its job profile both hold a value, the position wins. A typical Hire definition adds the Propose Compensation Hire and Change Organization Assignments subprocesses, a Review Employee Hire action, a Create Workday Account service step, and To Dos. Change Job and Termination have their own lessons. [[slnc 700]] Create Position: Prepares a Position Management organization. [[slnc 500]] Hiring Restrictions: Prepares a Job Management organization. [[slnc 500]] Hire: Brings a worker into a position or job. [[slnc 500]] Common Hire steps: Propose Compensation Hire, Change Organization Assignments, Review Employee Hire, Create Workday Account. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: If Hire is missing from an organization's Related Actions, the organization is not prepared: create a position (Position Management) or set hiring restrictions (Job Management). [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Change Job. [[slnc 500]] Change Job supports promotions, demotions, transfers, lateral moves, and changes to position data and location. Managers usually use Start Job Change, which asks simple questions from a change job template. Administrators use Transfer, Promote or Change Job, which shows a guided editor whose sections are secured by domain. Both run the Change Job business process. Maintain Change Job Templates sets the fields, sections, subprocesses, reasons, and help text for templates on Change Job, Change Location, Request Transfer, and Change Contingent Worker Details. Maintain Event Categories and Reasons maps your reasons to the delivered change types: Transfer, Promotion, Lateral Move, Demotion, and Data Change. Moving a manager can move all of their eligible teams or none, each through its own Assign Superior subprocess. Assign Roles - Change Assignments for Worker reviews roles, and it is not rescinded if Change Job is rescinded. [[slnc 700]] Start Job Change: Manager task with simple template questions. [[slnc 500]] Transfer, Promote or Change Job: Administrator task with the full guided editor. [[slnc 500]] Request Transfer: For a group without direct access to the worker. [[slnc 500]] Routing modifier: Sends a step to the current or proposed group member. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Change Job uses a template, so a group needs both the business process security policy (to start or act) and the domain policies (to see each section). Rescinding Change Job does not undo new role assignments. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Termination. [[slnc 500]] Termination ends the working relationship. Start it with Terminate Employee (worker Related Actions, then Job Change), through Submit Resignation (the Termination process must include the Review Worker Initiated Termination step), or through Severance. The termination date is the last day of employment; the worker shows as terminated just after midnight in their own time zone. Last Day of Work and Pay Through Date default to the termination date. You can record rehire eligibility, regrettable or not, a questionnaire such as an exit interview, notice periods, and attachments. Maintain Termination Categories holds customer-defined reasons with Involuntary, Retirement, Inactive, and Manager Reason flags. Typical steps: Manage Business Processes for Worker (reassign or cancel the worker's tasks), Assign Roles, and a Terminate User Account service step. Job overlap lets a replacement share the position for a time. No Show replaces termination for a hire who never starts. [[slnc 700]] Terminate Employee: Initiated by a manager or HR. [[slnc 500]] Submit Resignation: Worker-initiated, needs the Review Worker Initiated Termination step. [[slnc 500]] Manage Business Processes for Worker: Reassign or cancel the leaver’s tasks. [[slnc 500]] No Show: Hire never started, not counted in headcount. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Adding a group to the Termination business process policy lets it start the task, but it cannot see the Reason or Details sections until it is added to their domain policies (for example Staffing Actions: Termination Details). [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Compensation Basics. [[slnc 500]] A compensation package groups compensation grades and plans. A grade gives a pay range (minimum, midpoint, and 3, 4, or 5 segments) as guidance: a salary outside the range gives a warning, not an error. Grade profiles localize a grade, for example by country and currency. Plans hold pay components: salary, hourly, period salary, and unit salary for base pay, and allowance plans (amount, percent, or unit based). Compensation elements link plans to payroll earnings. Eligibility rules decide who is eligible for each component; two rules on one component use OR logic. Pay defaults during Hire (Propose Compensation Hire) and Change Job (Propose Compensation Change) only when the worker is eligible for exactly one package and grade, and the plan is in that package. [[slnc 700]] Package: Groups grades and plans. [[slnc 500]] Grade versus grade profile: Pay range versus localized range. [[slnc 500]] Eligibility rule: Who can receive a component. [[slnc 500]] Element: Links a plan to payroll earnings. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Eligible for two packages or two grades means nothing defaults. Eligible for a plan outside the package means it is selectable but does not default. [[slnc 1300]] Two retrieval questions to finish. [[slnc 800]] Question one.
