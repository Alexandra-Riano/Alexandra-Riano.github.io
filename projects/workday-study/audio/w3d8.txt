Week 3, day 8. Compensation basics. [[slnc 1200]] Today's objective is Compensation Basics, in the Staffing Setup domain. [[slnc 600]] In one line: See how packages, grades, plans, and eligibility rules default pay during staffing. [[slnc 1200]] Let's start with the mental model. [[slnc 500]] A compensation package groups compensation grades and plans. A grade gives a pay range (minimum, midpoint, and 3, 4, or 5 segments) as guidance: a salary outside the range gives a warning, not an error. Grade profiles localize a grade, for example by country and currency. Plans hold pay components: salary, hourly, period salary, and unit salary for base pay, and allowance plans (amount, percent, or unit based). Compensation elements link plans to payroll earnings. Eligibility rules decide who is eligible for each component; two rules on one component use OR logic. Pay defaults during Hire (Propose Compensation Hire) and Change Job (Propose Compensation Change) only when the worker is eligible for exactly one package and grade, and the plan is in that package. [[slnc 1200]] Now, the distinctions you need to know. [[slnc 500]] Package: Groups grades and plans. [[slnc 700]] Grade versus grade profile: Pay range versus localized range. [[slnc 700]] Eligibility rule: Who can receive a component. [[slnc 700]] Element: Links a plan to payroll earnings. [[slnc 700]]  [[slnc 600]] Here's how it shows up at work. [[slnc 500]] A worker is eligible for a car allowance plan that is not in their package. The plan does not default during Hire, but the compensation partner can add it by hand. [[slnc 1200]] Task and report names to recognize: Create Compensation Eligibility Rule, Create Compensation Grade, Create Salary Plan, Create Allowance Plan, Create Compensation Package, and Test Compensation Eligibility Rule. [[slnc 1200]] And the exam trap to watch for. [[slnc 500]] Eligible for two packages or two grades means nothing defaults. Eligible for a plan outside the package means it is selectable but does not default. [[slnc 1500]] Quick check. I'll read each question and the choices, then pause so you can answer out loud.
