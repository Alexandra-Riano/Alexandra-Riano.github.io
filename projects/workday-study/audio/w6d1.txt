Week 6, day 1. Condition rules. [[slnc 1200]] Today's objective is Condition Rules, in the Business Processes domain. [[slnc 600]] In one line: Distinguish entry, validation, while-running, and exit conditions. [[slnc 1200]] Let's start with the mental model. [[slnc 500]] Entry decides whether a non-initiation step begins. Validation blocks progress on Initiation or Action. While Running determines whether the next approval-chain member acts. Exit determines whether supported approval-chain or integration steps can complete. [[slnc 1200]] Now, the distinctions you need to know. [[slnc 500]] Entry: Should the step start?. [[slnc 700]] Validation: May the process continue?. [[slnc 700]] While Running: Does the next chain member act?. [[slnc 700]] Exit: May the running step complete?. [[slnc 700]]  [[slnc 600]] Here's how it shows up at work. [[slnc 500]] Run executive approval only above a compensation threshold by attaching an entry condition. [[slnc 1200]] Task and report names to recognize: Create Condition Rule, Maintain Step Conditions, and Condition Rule Tester. [[slnc 1200]] And the exam trap to watch for. [[slnc 500]] The four types are not interchangeable and are not available on every step type. [[slnc 1500]] Quick check. I'll read each question and the choices, then pause so you can answer out loud. [[slnc 1000]] Question one. [[slnc 400]] Which condition type decides whether a non-initiation step starts? [[slnc 700]] A: While Running. [[slnc 450]] B: Exit. [[slnc 450]] C: Validation. [[slnc 450]] D: Entry. [[slnc 450]] Take a moment. [[slnc 5000]] The answer is D: Entry. [[slnc 500]] Entry decides if the step begins or is skipped. [[slnc 1400]] Question two. [[slnc 400]] Which condition type blocks the process on an Initiation or Action step? [[slnc 700]] A: Exit. [[slnc 500]] Validation stops progress until the data is valid. [[slnc 1400]] Question three. [[slnc 400]] Which condition type decides whether the next member of an approval chain acts?
