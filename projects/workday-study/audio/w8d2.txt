[[slnc 1000]] This week's goal: Finish policy configuration, integrate every domain, and simulate exam day. [[slnc 1200]] Recap: Configurable Security Framework. [[slnc 500]] Users become members of security groups. Groups receive permissions through domain or business-process policies. Domains group securable tasks, reports, data sources, fields, and integrations. business process policies govern participation in event types. [[slnc 700]] Domain policy: View or modify secured items. [[slnc 500]] business process policy: Initiate or act on events. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Creating a group or assigning a role does not grant access until a relevant policy gives permission. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Security Group Types. [[slnc 500]] Workday-delivered groups have Workday-maintained criteria. Role-based groups derive membership from assignable roles and can be constrained or unconstrained. User-based groups use explicitly maintained membership. Constraints determine the target population. [[slnc 700]] Role-based constrained: Role membership plus supported context. [[slnc 500]] User-based: Named maintained membership. [[slnc 500]] Workday-delivered: Workday-owned criteria. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Wrong constraint context can create broad access even when membership is correct. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Domain versus business process Security Policies. [[slnc 500]] Domain policies grant View or Modify access to items and may inherit parent permissions. business process policies control initiation, action, approval, delegation, cancel, rescind, deny, and similar event actions. Saved changes remain pending until activated. [[slnc 700]] Can see/modify item?: Domain policy. [[slnc 500]] Can initiate/approve event?: business process policy. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Saving a policy edit does not activate it. Review, activate, and test with suitable accounts. [[slnc 1300]] Two retrieval questions to finish.
