[[slnc 1000]] This week's goal: Finish policy configuration, integrate every domain, and simulate exam day. [[slnc 1200]] Recap: Security Group Types. [[slnc 500]] Groups get members three ways: Workday-assigned (Workday defines and maintains members, such as Employee As Self and All Employees), manually assigned (administrators assign users, as in user-based groups), and derived (membership follows criteria such as role assignments, as in role-based groups). Role-based groups can be constrained or unconstrained. The context type describes what target data members can reach: unconstrained (no organizational limit), constrained to their own instance (self-service groups), constrained to public data, or constrained to the organizations where the member holds the role or belongs. A user’s access is the union of all their groups’ permissions. [[slnc 700]] Role-based constrained: Role membership plus supported context. [[slnc 500]] User-based: Named maintained membership. [[slnc 500]] Workday-delivered: Workday-owned criteria. [[slnc 500]] Membership methods: Workday-assigned, manually assigned, derived. [[slnc 500]] Context type: What target data members can reach. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Wrong constraint context can create broad access even when membership is correct. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Advanced Security Group Types. [[slnc 500]] Some groups derive members from worker data. Job-based groups use one job criterion, such as job profile, job family, or management level, and can be constrained to the member's own organizations or unconstrained. Location membership groups include workers at chosen locations and are always unconstrained. Organization membership groups include members of chosen organizations, including hierarchies, and can be constrained or unconstrained. Combination groups reuse other groups. Aggregation: members of any included group, each keeping its own constraints. Intersection: members of all included groups, with constraints intersected; it cannot include intersection, rule-based, or integration system groups. Segment-based: controls access to segments of values, such as document categories. Rule-based groups filter a baseline group with a security rule; changing the rule needs no activation. Service center and integration system groups give access to non-workers. [[slnc 700]] Aggregation: Any included group (union). [[slnc 500]] Intersection: All included groups, constraints intersected. [[slnc 500]] Rule-based: Baseline group filtered by a security rule. [[slnc 500]] Integration system: Access for integration system users (ISUs). [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Location membership groups are always unconstrained. To limit what members see by location, use an organization membership (constrained) group on a location hierarchy. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Domain versus business process Security Policies. [[slnc 500]] Domain policies grant View or Modify (and Get or Put for integrations) to items in a domain; Modify includes View, Put includes Get, and child domains inherit from their parent. Each business process type has one business process policy, shared by all of its definitions, that controls who can initiate, act, approve, delegate, cancel, rescind, deny, and more. Subprocesses do not inherit the parent's policy. Policy changes stay pending until you run Activate Pending Security Policy Changes; Activate Previous Security Timestamp reverts. Changes to security group membership take effect immediately. Template-driven processes such as Termination and Change Job need both the business process policy and the domain policies for each section of the page. [[slnc 700]] Can see or modify an item?: Domain policy. [[slnc 500]] Can initiate or approve an event?: business process policy. [[slnc 500]] Template-driven process: Needs both business process and domain permissions. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Saving a policy edit does not activate it.
