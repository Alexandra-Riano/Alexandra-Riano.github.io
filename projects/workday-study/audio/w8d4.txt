[[slnc 1000]] This week's goal: Finish policy configuration, integrate every domain, and simulate exam day. [[slnc 1200]] Recap: Configurable Security Framework. [[slnc 500]] Users become members of security groups. Groups receive permissions through domain or business-process policies. Domains group securable tasks, reports, data sources, fields, and integrations. business process policies govern participation in event types. [[slnc 700]] Domain policy: View or modify secured items. [[slnc 500]] business process policy: Initiate or act on events. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Creating a group or assigning a role does not grant access until a relevant policy gives permission. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Security Group Types. [[slnc 500]] Groups get members three ways: Workday-assigned (Workday defines and maintains members, such as Employee As Self and All Employees), manually assigned (administrators assign users, as in user-based groups), and derived (membership follows criteria such as role assignments, as in role-based groups). Role-based groups can be constrained or unconstrained. The context type describes what target data members can reach: unconstrained (no organizational limit), constrained to their own instance (self-service groups), constrained to public data, or constrained to the organizations where the member holds the role or belongs. A user’s access is the union of all their groups’ permissions. [[slnc 700]] Role-based constrained: Role membership plus supported context. [[slnc 500]] User-based: Named maintained membership. [[slnc 500]] Workday-delivered: Workday-owned criteria. [[slnc 500]] Membership methods: Workday-assigned, manually assigned, derived. [[slnc 500]] Context type: What target data members can reach. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Wrong constraint context can create broad access even when membership is correct. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Domain versus business process Security Policies. [[slnc 500]] Domain policies grant View or Modify (and Get or Put for integrations) to items in a domain; Modify includes View, Put includes Get, and child domains inherit from their parent. Each business process type has one business process policy, shared by all of its definitions, that controls who can initiate, act, approve, delegate, cancel, rescind, deny, and more. Subprocesses do not inherit the parent's policy. Policy changes stay pending until you run Activate Pending Security Policy Changes; Activate Previous Security Timestamp reverts. Changes to security group membership take effect immediately. Template-driven processes such as Termination and Change Job need both the business process policy and the domain policies for each section of the page. [[slnc 700]] Can see or modify an item?: Domain policy. [[slnc 500]] Can initiate or approve an event?: business process policy. [[slnc 500]] Template-driven process: Needs both business process and domain permissions. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Saving a policy edit does not activate it.
