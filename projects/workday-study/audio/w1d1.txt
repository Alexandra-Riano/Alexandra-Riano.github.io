Week 1, day 1. Study Job Families  and  Groups. [[slnc 1200]] Today's objective is Job Families  and  Groups, in the Staffing Setup domain. [[slnc 600]] In one line: Organize job profiles into optional reporting and filtering hierarchies. [[slnc 1200]] Let's start with the mental model. [[slnc 500]] A job profile may belong to one or more job families; a family can belong to a family group. Families can support prompt filters, reports, Recruiting categories, condition rules, and eligibility rules. [[slnc 1200]] Now, the distinctions you need to know. [[slnc 500]] Job family: Groups related profiles. [[slnc 700]] Job family group: Groups families into a broader catalog layer. [[slnc 700]]  [[slnc 600]] Here's how it shows up at work. [[slnc 500]] A compensation rule uses the Engineering family. Add a new Software Engineer profile to that family or the expected default may not apply. [[slnc 1200]] Task and report names to recognize: Create Job Family, Job Family report, Edit Job Profile, and Job Catalog. [[slnc 1200]] And the exam trap to watch for. [[slnc 500]] A family classifies work; it is not a supervisory organization or staffing model. [[slnc 1500]] Quick check. I'll read each question and the choices, then pause so you can answer out loud. [[slnc 1000]] Question one. [[slnc 400]] How many job families can a single job profile belong to? [[slnc 700]] A: None; only job family groups hold profiles. [[slnc 450]] B: One or more job families. [[slnc 450]] C: As many as its management level allows. [[slnc 450]] D: Exactly one job family. [[slnc 450]] Take a moment. [[slnc 5000]] The answer is B: One or more job families. [[slnc 500]] A job profile can be assigned to one or more job families. [[slnc 1400]] Question two. [[slnc 400]] Are job families required when you create a job profile? [[slnc 700]] A: Yes, every profile needs a job family. [[slnc 450]] B: Only when the profile has a compensation grade. [[slnc 450]] C: Only in Position Management organizations. [[slnc 450]] D: No, job families and groups are optional. [[slnc 450]] Take a moment. [[slnc 5000]] The answer is D: No, job families and groups are optional. [[slnc 500]] Job families and job family groups are optional ways to categorize profiles. [[slnc 1400]] Question three. [[slnc 400]] A worker is hired into a job profile that is not in the job family used by a compensation eligibility rule. What happens? [[slnc 700]] A: Workday adds the profile to the family automatically. [[slnc 450]] B: The compensation package does not default during the hire. [[slnc 450]] C: The hire is blocked until the family is fixed. [[slnc 450]] D: The worker moves to a different supervisory organization. [[slnc 450]] Take a moment. [[slnc 5000]] The answer is B: The compensation package does not default during the hire. [[slnc 500]] Eligibility rules only see profiles in the family they reference, so compensation won't default.
