[[slnc 1000]] This week's goal: Build a durable model of types, definitions, events, steps, and completion. [[slnc 1200]] Recap: business process Terminology  and  Framework. [[slnc 500]] A business process type is the event category; a definition is its configured workflow; an event is one transaction running through that definition. Definitions specify steps, order, responsible security groups, conditions, notifications, and completion. [[slnc 700]] Type: Event category. [[slnc 500]] Definition: Configured workflow. [[slnc 500]] Event: One running transaction. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Do not confuse the reusable definition with one running event. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Update business process Definitions. [[slnc 500]] Default definitions support common routing; organization-specific definitions handle organizations whose steps differ greatly from the default; condition rules on individual steps control whether they run. Workday looks for the initiating organization's definition, then its superior organizations, then the default. The completion step commits data and sets Successfully Completed. [[slnc 700]] Default: Common workflow for all organizations. [[slnc 500]] Organization-specific: Own steps for a target org and its subordinates. [[slnc 500]] Condition rules: Decide whether an individual step runs. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: All approvals and reviews must precede the completion step, though later To Dos may remain. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Business Process Step Types. [[slnc 500]] Initiation starts the event; Action performs Workday work or a subprocess; Approval records a decision; Service invokes automation; To Do instructs a person to complete work that Workday might not validate. [[slnc 700]] Approval: Formal decision. [[slnc 500]] Action: Task, review, or subprocess. [[slnc 500]] Service: Automation. [[slnc 500]] To Do: Human follow-up. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Completing a To Do proves the click, not necessarily the external activity. [[slnc 1300]] Two retrieval questions to finish. [[slnc 800]] Question one. [[slnc 500]] Customers can't add types, only configure definitions. [[slnc 1400]] Question two. [[slnc 400]] An org has no definition of its own, but its superior org does. Which definition is used? [[slnc 700]] A: The most recently created definition. [[slnc 450]] B: The default definition. [[slnc 450]] C: None; the event fails. [[slnc 450]] D: The superior org's definition. [[slnc 450]] Take a moment. [[slnc 5000]] The answer is D: The superior org's definition. [[slnc 500]] Workday checks the org, then its superiors, then the default.
