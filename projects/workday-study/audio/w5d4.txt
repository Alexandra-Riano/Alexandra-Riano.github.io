[[slnc 1000]] This week's goal: Build a durable model of types, definitions, events, steps, and completion. [[slnc 1200]] Recap: business process Terminology  and  Framework. [[slnc 500]] A business process type is the event category; a definition is its configured workflow; an event is one transaction running through that definition. Definitions specify steps, order, responsible security groups, conditions, notifications, and completion. [[slnc 700]] Type: Event category. [[slnc 500]] Definition: Configured workflow. [[slnc 500]] Event: One running transaction. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Do not confuse the reusable definition with one running event. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Update business process Definitions. [[slnc 500]] Workday delivers business process types; you configure definitions. Workday recommends one default definition because it is easiest to maintain. Use an organization-specific definition when an organization's steps differ greatly; it covers that organization and its subordinates. Use rule-based definitions when the same condition rule would sit on three or more steps, or when the path depends on who initiates or where workers are. Each rule-based definition has one condition rule, which Workday evaluates once, at initiation, to pick the definition. If no rule is met, Workday uses the organization-specific definition for the organization or its nearest superior, then the default. Definitions with errors or that are inactive are skipped. The completion step commits the data and sets Successfully Completed. A process has only one, and all approvals and reviews must come before it or be it. [[slnc 700]] Default: Recommended and easiest to maintain. [[slnc 500]] Organization-specific: Own steps for an organization whose flow differs greatly. [[slnc 500]] Rule-based: One condition rule, checked once at initiation, picks the definition. [[slnc 500]] Completion step: Commits data, and approvals and reviews come first. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: A rule-based definition uses exactly one condition rule (it can nest conditions) and only fields that have a value at initiation. Approvals and reviews never come after the completion step. [[slnc 1300]] Recap: Business Process Step Types. [[slnc 500]] Initiation starts the event and is always first. Action steps either prompt a review, where the reviewer can edit and approve, or start a subprocess. Approval steps approve, send back, or deny the whole process; approvers cannot edit, and a denial denies the entire process. Service steps run Workday-delivered automation such as Create Workday Account. To Do steps send an Inbox reminder for work inside or outside Workday; Workday delivers none, so you create them with Maintain To Dos. Approval chains move up the management chain until the top or an exit condition. A consolidated approval combines several approvals for the same worker into one task, but only for steps before it. [[slnc 700]] Approval: Formal decision, no editing. [[slnc 500]] Action: A review (with editing) or a subprocess. [[slnc 500]] Service: Workday-delivered automation. [[slnc 500]] To Do: Reminder for work Workday may not check. [[slnc 500]] Remember the trap: Completing a To Do proves the click, not necessarily the external activity.
